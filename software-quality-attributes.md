## Categories / Acronyms

### agility

Domain: Software Development

Quality attributes: Debuggability, Extensibility, Portability, Scalability, Securability, Testability, Understandability

### RASUI

Domain: Software Quality

Quality attributes: Availability, Installability, Reliability, Serviceability/Supportability, Usability

### FURPS

Domain: Requirements Engineering

Quality attributes: Functionality, Performance, Reliability, Serviceability/Supportability, Usability

### RASR

Domain: Databases

Quality attributes: Availability, Recoverability, Reliability, Scalability

### ACID

Domain: Databases

Quality attributes: Atomicity, Consistency, Durability, Integrity

### RAMS

Domain: Safety-critical systems

Quality attributes: Availability, Maintainability, Reliability, Safety

## Quality Attributes

### Accessibility

The degree to which the software is usable by people with disabilities, often through assistive technologies.

Positive effects on: Usability, Availability, Localizability, Understandability
Negative effects on: Complexity, Securability

### Accountability

The degree to which the software keeps accurate records and has the ability to explain its operations.

Positive effects on: Credibility, Integrity, Auditability, Traceability, Reliability, Correctness
Negative effects on: Autonomy, Flexibility, Privacy, Complexity, Affordability

### Accuracy

The capability of the software to retrieve and rank data accurately according to given specifications.


### Adaptability

The degree to which the software adapts its behavior based on information about its users and environment.


### Administrability

The extent to which users with elevated permissions can effectively configure the software.


### Affordability

The cost of the software as it relates to what the purchaser can afford.


### Atomicity

In databases, the assurance that transactions are treated as single units that either fully succeed or fail.

Category / acronym: ACID

### Auditability

The degree to which the software logs actions and allows authorized inspection of these logs.


### Autonomy

The degree to which service-oriented software operates independently from its execution environment.


### Availability

The degree to which the software is ready and available for use.

Category / acronym: RASUI, RASR, RAMS

### Capabilities

The range of actions, tasks, or functions that the software can perform.


### Compatibility

The degree to which the software can work effectively with other systems or software.


### Complexity

The degree of difficulty to understand, modify, or maintain the software.


### Composability

The degree to which the software's components can be assembled and reassembled.


### Confidentiality

The degree to which the software protects sensitive information from unauthorized access.


### Configurability

The extent to which settings and features within the software can be customized by the user.


### Consistency

The degree to which the software behaves and presents itself uniformly across different scenarios.

Category / acronym: ACID

### Correctness

The extent to which the software performs its functions without error.


### Credibility

The degree to which the software is reliable and induces trust.


### Customizability

The degree to which the software allows the user to tailor features to their specific needs.


### Debuggability

The ease with which bugs can be discovered and fixed within the software.

Category / acronym: agility

### Degradability

The degree to which the software can gracefully reduce its functionality in the face of system constraints.


### Demonstrability

The ease with which the software's functions and capabilities can be demonstrated to others.


### Dependability

The extent to which the software can be relied upon to perform its functions accurately and consistently.

Positive effects on: Availability, Reliability, Safety, Integrity, Maintainability

### Deployability

The degree to which the software can be easily installed, configured, and put into operation.


### Determinability

The extent to which the software's behavior can be predictably determined from its inputs and conditions.


### Discoverability

The degree to which the software's features and functionalities are easily discoverable by users.


### Distributability

The extent to which the software can be distributed across multiple platforms and environments.


### Durability

The degree to which the software can endure stress and continue to function over extended periods of time.

Category / acronym: ACID

### Effectiveness

The ability of the software to achieve its goals and produce desired outcomes.


### Efficiency

The degree to which the software performs its functions without wasting resources.


### Evolvability

The extent to which the software can adapt to future changes in its environment or requirements.


### Extensibility

The degree to which new features or functionalities can be added to the existing software.

Category / acronym: agility

### Failure Transparency

The extent to which the software reveals or conceals its failures and the impact of those failures.


### Fault-Tolerance

The degree to which the software continues to function in the presence of errors or failures.


### Fidelity

The degree to which the software accurately represents or simulates real-world conditions or data.


### Flexibility

The ease with which the software can adapt to different tasks or scenarios.


### Functionality

The range and capabilities of tasks that the software can perform.

Category / acronym: FURPS

### Inspectability

The degree to which the software’s internal workings can be examined for debugging or analysis.


### Installability

The ease with which the software can be installed and set up for use.

Category / acronym: RASUI

### Integrity

The degree to which the software prevents unauthorized access and maintains the accuracy of data.

Category / acronym: ACID
Positive effects on: Securability, Survivability

### Interchangeability

The ease with which components within the software can be exchanged.


### Interoperability

The degree to which the software can interact with other systems or software components.


### Learnability

The ease with which new users can understand and use the software.


### Localizability

The ability of the software to be adapted for different languages, regions, and cultures.


### Maintainability

The ease with which the software can be modified, updated, or corrected.

Category / acronym: RAMS

### Manageability

The extent to which the software can be efficiently managed and monitored.


### Mobility

The ability of the software to operate effectively on different devices or environments.


### Modifiability

The degree to which the software can be easily changed to add new features or fix issues.


### Modularity

The extent to which the software is composed of independent, interchangeable modules.


### Observability

The degree to which internal states of the software can be observed through outputs.


### Operability

The ease with which the software can be kept in a functioning state.


### Orthogonality

The independence between different features or components of the software.


### Performance

The effectiveness of the software in terms of speed, efficiency, and resource usage.

Category / acronym: FURPS

### Portability

The ease with which the software can be moved from one environment to another.

Category / acronym: agility

### Precision

The accuracy with which the software performs its calculations or processes.


### Predictability

The degree to which the software's behavior can be anticipated.


### Privacy

The degree to which the software ensures that unauthorized users cannot access, modify, or delete personal information.


### Producibility

The ease with which the software can be reliably reproduced or replicated.


### Provability

The extent to which formal methods can be used to prove the software's correctness.


### Recoverability

The ability of the software to restore its state and functionality after failure.

Category / acronym: RASR

### Relevance

The degree to which the software meets the real-world needs of its users.


### Reliability

The ability of the software to function correctly and consistently.

Category / acronym: RASUI, FURPS, RASR, RAMS

### Repeatability

The degree to which the software provides the same output for the same input under the same conditions.


### Reproducibility

The ability to get the same results consistently across different environments and conditions.


### Resilience

The ability of the software to adapt and recover from adversity.


### Responsiveness

The software's ability to quickly respond to user input or system events.


### Reusability

The extent to which components of the software can be used in different contexts.


### Robustness

The ability of the software to cope with errors and unexpected conditions.


### Safety

The degree to which the software avoids causing harm to users or the environment.

Category / acronym: RAMS

### Scalability

The software's ability to handle increased loads efficiently.

Category / acronym: agility, RASR

### Seamlessness

The degree to which the software integrates its various components without visible gaps or inconsistencies.


### Securability

The ability of the software to protect against unauthorized access and data breaches.

Category / acronym: agility
Positive effects on: Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability, Dependability

### Self-Sustainability

The software's ability to maintain its operational state without external intervention.


### Serviceability/Supportability

The ease with which the software can be serviced, maintained, and supported.

Category / acronym: RASUI, FURPS

### Simplicity

The degree to which the software is easy to understand and use.


### Stability

The extent to which the software remains reliable under different conditions.


### Standards Compliance

The degree to which the software adheres to industry or regulatory standards.


### Survivability

The ability of the software to endure and recover from catastrophic failures.


### Sustainability

The ability of the software to endure over time while meeting environmental and social requirements.


### Tailorability

The ease with which the software can be customized to meet specific user needs.


### Testability

The degree to which the software can be effectively tested to ensure it meets its requirements.

Category / acronym: agility

### Timeliness

The software's ability to perform tasks or provide responses within an acceptable time frame.


### Traceability

The ability to track changes, data flow, or user actions throughout the software.


### Transparency

The degree to which the software's actions and decision-making processes are visible to the user.


### Ubiquity

The extent to which the software is available and functional across various platforms and devices.


### Understandability

The ease with which users can comprehend the software's functions and use.

Category / acronym: agility

### Upgradability

The ease with which new features can be added or existing features can be modified.


### Usability

The degree to which the software is easy to use and provides a good user experience.

Category / acronym: RASUI, FURPS

### Vulnerability

The extent to which the software is susceptible to unauthorized access or exploitation.
